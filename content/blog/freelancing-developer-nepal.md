Nepal's IT sector has grown fast. One industry estimate puts IT service exports at around **USD 1 billion in 2025**, more than double 2022 levels ([NecoJobs](https://www.necojobs.com.np/blogs/career-tips/nepal-job-market-trends)), and better internet in Kathmandu, Pokhara and other cities makes remote work realistic for many developers.

I've spent the last two years freelancing — building news portals, education platforms, travel sites and e-commerce stores. Here's what I'd tell a developer starting today.

## 1. Build proof, not just a CV

Clients don't hire degrees; they hire evidence that you can deliver. Before chasing clients:

- Build **3–5 real-looking projects** with live demos (a business site, a dashboard, an e-commerce flow).
- Write a short case study for each: problem, what you built, tech stack, result.
- Put them on a fast, well-designed portfolio with your own domain.

Your portfolio *is* your sales pitch. A slow, broken portfolio tells clients exactly how their project will turn out.

## 2. Start local, then go global

Local businesses — consultancies, schools, travel agencies, restaurants, news outlets — need websites and often prefer someone they can meet. Early local projects give you:

- real testimonials and live sites to show
- experience with real requirements and deadlines
- referrals — satisfied local clients are the best source of new work

Then use that portfolio to reach international clients through LinkedIn, freelance platforms and your network.

## 3. Pick a specialisation

"I do everything" is hard to sell. "I build fast, SEO-friendly Next.js websites for education consultancies" is easy to understand and easy to refer. You can still take other work — but lead with a clear offer.

## 4. Pricing: stop charging by the hour (mostly)

Local clients usually prefer **fixed-price projects**. To price well:

1. Break the project into features and estimate each.
2. Add 20–30% for communication, revisions and surprises.
3. Write down exactly what's included (pages, features, revision rounds).
4. Charge separately for hosting, domain, maintenance and content entry.

For international clients, research market rates for your stack and region — underpricing makes you look less experienced, not more attractive.

## 5. Contracts and payments

Even for small projects, write a simple agreement covering scope, timeline, price, payment schedule and who owns the code.

- Ask for **an advance** (30–50%) before starting.
- Tie remaining payments to milestones.
- Hand over the final source code and admin access after the final payment.

For international payments, compare the receiving options available to Nepali freelancers (bank transfers and payment services) and their fees and exchange rates. Keep records for tax purposes, and check current Nepal Rastra Bank and tax rules — they change, and a local accountant is worth the cost.

## 6. Communication is the real skill

Most freelance projects fail because of communication, not code.

- Confirm requirements in writing.
- Send short progress updates (with preview links) at least weekly.
- Say "no" or "that's extra" politely and early.
- Respond within a day, even if it's just "I'll check and reply tomorrow".

## 7. Keep learning — strategically

The market in 2026 rewards developers who can:

- build full stack apps (Next.js + a solid backend)
- handle deployment, performance and SEO
- use AI coding agents effectively **and** review their output critically
- understand security basics

I write about these topics on this blog — start with [AI coding agents](/blog/ai-coding-agents-2026) and [SEO for AI search](/blog/seo-for-ai-search-2026).

## 8. Protect your time and health

Freelancing can blur work and life. Set working hours, take breaks, and don't accept every project. Burnout will cost you more than any single client pays.

## Key takeaways

- Proof beats promises: build and showcase real projects.
- Start with local clients and referrals, then expand internationally.
- Specialise, price by project, and use simple written contracts with advances.
- Over-communicate, keep learning, and protect your health.
